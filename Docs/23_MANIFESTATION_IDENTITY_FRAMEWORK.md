# Manifestation Identity Framework (Draft)

> Status: living design framework.
>
> This document extends `07_MANIFESTATION_SYSTEM.md`. It is intentionally incomplete and should evolve as individual manifestations are developed, tested, and approved.

---

## Purpose

Manifestations are not merely visual effects or elemental constructs.

They are localized changes in reality that express the inner nature of an individual.

A manifestation should remain recognizable because of how it behaves, how it alters its surroundings, and what emotional truth it carries—not because it repeats the same shape, weapon, color, or material in every appearance.

Identity exists beneath appearance.

---

## Core Principle

A manifestation may change its:

- medium,
- color,
- density,
- texture,
- visibility,
- scale,
- intensity,
- composition,
- and degree of environmental influence.

It should not lose its underlying language.

The goal is consistency through behavior rather than repetition through appearance.

Behavior comes before geometry.

Identity comes before spectacle.

Reality changes before magic appears.

---

## Manifestation DNA

Each individual manifestation should eventually define a distinct identity profile. The categories below are a working framework rather than a fixed schema.

### Reality Behavior

Describe what reality tends to do while the manifestation is present.

Possible behaviors include:

- suppression,
- expansion,
- fracture,
- inversion,
- suspension,
- repetition,
- erosion,
- delay,
- acceleration,
- stillness,
- displacement,
- or contradiction.

This should describe a recurring tendency, not a mandatory visual effect.

### Motion Grammar

Describe how the manifestation moves regardless of its current medium.

Consider:

- rhythm,
- continuity,
- acceleration,
- hesitation,
- direction,
- weight,
- recoil,
- flow,
- interruption,
- and response to the user's movement.

A recognizable motion grammar should survive changes in material and scale.

### Spatial Language

Describe how the manifestation occupies or alters space.

Questions may include:

- Does it gather, spread, fold, trail, collapse, or orbit?
- Does it create absence or fill it?
- Does it follow architecture or ignore it?
- Does it remain close to the user or affect distant parts of the environment?
- Does it reveal shapes directly or allow them to emerge through implication and negative space?

### Emotional Grammar

A manifestation should communicate an emotional truth through physical behavior.

This is not limited to naming an emotion. It should describe how that emotional quality becomes visible through:

- timing,
- pressure,
- restraint,
- instability,
- repetition,
- proximity,
- interruption,
- or environmental response.

The same emotion may appear differently at different levels of control, exhaustion, or intent.

### Environmental Consequences

The surrounding world should participate in the manifestation.

Possible interactions include changes to:

- wind,
- rain,
- dust,
- heat,
- sound,
- light,
- reflections,
- fabric,
- foliage,
- gravity,
- residue,
- architecture,
- or the motion of nearby objects.

Environmental consequences often communicate identity more effectively than a central magical construct.

The environment should remain legible and retain its own identity unless the scene specifically calls for overwhelming transformation.

### Recognition Motifs

Recognition motifs are small recurring ideas that help identify a manifestation without forcing literal repetition.

A motif may be:

- a motion tendency,
- a recurring relationship to negative space,
- a specific kind of environmental silence,
- an interruption in gravity,
- an incomplete arc,
- a delayed reflection,
- a material transition,
- a recurring sense of pressure,
- or another restrained behavioral cue.

Motifs should remain flexible enough to appear differently across scenes.

---

## Identity Layers

### Immutable or Rarely Changed

These define the manifestation's identity and should remain stable unless the character undergoes a major canonical transformation:

- reality behavior,
- motion grammar,
- emotional grammar,
- spatial language,
- core recognition motifs,
- relationship to the environment.

### Flexible

These define the manifestation's expression in a particular moment:

- medium,
- texture,
- color,
- density,
- visibility,
- scale,
- intensity,
- residue,
- degree of abstraction,
- and how directly a recognizable form appears.

### Scene-Dependent

These belong to the specific image or sequence:

- camera,
- composition,
- weather,
- lighting,
- location,
- action,
- emotional context,
- level of control,
- and narrative purpose.

---

## Medium as Vocabulary

A manifestation should not be reduced to a single permanent material unless canon specifically requires it.

Instead, each manifestation may have a vocabulary of:

- primary media,
- secondary media,
- transient media,
- environmental carriers,
- residue,
- and failure states.

A medium should govern motion, texture, rigidity, utility, residue, and environmental interaction. It should not be treated as a cosmetic skin applied to the same repeated construct.

Different scenes may emphasize different parts of the vocabulary while preserving the manifestation's deeper identity.

---

## Form Through Implication

When a manifestation resembles a weapon, creature, symbol, or other recognizable form, the form does not always need to exist as a solid object.

It may emerge through:

- negative space,
- intersecting currents,
- temporary alignment,
- environmental reaction,
- residue,
- shadow,
- absence,
- reflected motion,
- or the viewer's interpretation.

The viewer may recognize the idea of a form before the image fully defines it.

This is preferred when a hard construct would weaken the intended medium, surrealism, or personal identity of the manifestation.

---

## Surrealism Without Visual Collapse

Awe should come from impossible behavior, not simply from increased detail.

Surrealism may be created by allowing one or more local physical expectations to fail while preserving the quality and legibility of the surrounding scene.

Examples include:

- wind moving in contradictory directions,
- ash remaining still while fabric moves,
- reflections lagging behind their sources,
- light avoiding a specific region,
- gravity changing only for selected materials,
- sound appearing visually absent from a space,
- or a shape becoming recognizable only through temporary environmental alignment.

Avoid using excessive sharpness, micro-detail, particle density, glowing ornament, or procedural clutter as substitutes for awe.

---

## Variation Without Identity Loss

Two appearances of the same manifestation should not need to share the same:

- silhouette,
- material balance,
- color,
- scale,
- framing,
- or level of visibility.

They should still feel related through shared behavioral rules.

Variation is successful when the manifestation adapts to the scene without becoming generic elemental bending, a repeated hard construct, or a decorative overlay.

---

## Recognition Test

Ask:

> If the character were removed from the image, would someone familiar with Advent Accord still recognize whose manifestation this is?

Additional tests:

- Does the manifestation remain identifiable without relying on its signature color?
- Does it still feel correct when the primary medium changes?
- Is the environment participating rather than merely sitting behind an effect?
- Does the scene preserve painterly rendering and environmental quality?
- Is awe coming from impossible behavior rather than detail accumulation?
- Is the recognizable form suggested where appropriate rather than forced into rigidity?
- Could this effect be reassigned to another character without substantial changes

If the final answer is yes, the identity is probably not yet specific enough.

---

## Common Failure Modes

Reject or revise manifestations that default to:

- a hard construct when the medium should remain fluid, particulate, atmospheric, or ambiguous,
- one repeated weapon or silhouette in every scene,
- generic elemental bending,
- a single material with no variation in texture, density, state, or environmental behavior,
- sharpness and micro-detail increasing as the manifestation is refined,
- the manifestation replacing the established art style,
- excessive particles or decorative noise,
- the environment losing quality or identity,
- color alone carrying character recognition,
- simplified effects that ignore motion grammar,
- or spectacle that overwhelms emotional and narrative purpose.

## Revision Policy

This framework is expected to change as manifestations are discovered through writing, concept development, generation tests, and approved visual references.

Individual manifestation identities should not be treated as finalized until their immutable traits, flexible traits, recognition motifs, and forbidden patterns have been explicitly reviewed.

Changes that affect established character identity or manifestation canon should follow the repository's canon-approval process.
