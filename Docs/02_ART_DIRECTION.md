# Art Direction

## Core Identity
Advent Accord should read as a mature painterly-dimensional illustration rather
than a rendered 3D scene, generic anime frame, polite adventure cartoon, or
hyper-detailed concept-art composite.

## Current Aesthetic Authority

The current renderer authority is a three-image core:

1. the beautiful seated woman beside a koi pond;
2. the original intricate dark-coat full-body man;
3. the original seated pendant woman.

These references define a realm rather than a single repeated subject. Preserve
their rendering intelligence, not their recurring people, clothing, gardens,
windows, palettes, poses, or architecture.

Core traits:

- softly modeled dimensional faces;
- stylized but anatomically grounded facial construction;
- mature and emotionally specific expressions;
- romantic or dramatic shaped light with readable shadow interiors;
- rich painterly transitions across skin, fabric, hair, architecture, and
  atmosphere;
- intriguing clothing and material construction;
- beauty, atmosphere, tension, and narrative presence;
- controlled intricacy and selective edge hierarchy;
- cohesive but widely variable color;
- neither photographic/PBR nor flat/cartoon rendering.

## Primary Qualities
- Painterly brush economy
- Medium-frequency detail
- Strong composition
- Large value masses
- Selective complexity
- Clear focal hierarchy
- Rich but controlled color
- Cinematic light
- Environmental storytelling
- Lived-in spaces
- Mature emotional specificity
- Wide palette and lighting behavior
- Minimal dependence on dark outlines

## Evaluation Order

Judge a visual in this order. Do not average all qualities into one vague
"looks good" score.

1. **Anatomy and pose readability:** the body must be structurally credible,
   grounded, and free of distracting hand, limb, proportion, or contact errors.
2. **Character design and maturity:** the person must feel authored, specific,
   and appropriate to their age, role, culture, and emotional state. Attractive
   characters may be beautiful without becoming generically youthful,
   cosmetically perfect, or interchangeable.
3. **Dimensional rendering:** faces, bodies, garments, and important objects
   must turn through light and shadow rather than depend on outlines or flat
   local color.
4. **Composition and narrative relationship:** pose, gaze, spacing, props, and
   camera placement must communicate what is happening.
5. **Environment identity and density:** the setting should feel particular,
   useful, inhabited, and lived in, with enough objects and spatial layers to
   imply activity and history.
6. **Color and lighting cohesion:** character and setting must share one
   believable light and color world. Color variety is encouraged, but the
   image should not fragment into unrelated accents.
7. **Selective detail and finish:** detail should reward attention at focal
   areas and explain material, construction, culture, or story.

The first three are threshold qualities. Strong environment density, color, or
ornament does not excuse weak anatomy, generic character design, or flat form.
Conversely, a strong character in an empty or generic room is not a complete
success. Record these axes separately when comparing candidates.

## Character Standard

Characters are the primary stress test because weak anatomy or generic design
is immediately visible even when an environment is attractive.

Prefer:

- believable skeletal structure, weight, balance, and hand-object contact;
- mature facial planes and age-specific features;
- distinct silhouettes, grooming, clothing construction, and occupational
  evidence;
- beauty with individuality rather than standardized prettiness;
- expressions with restraint, ambiguity, tension, warmth, fatigue, authority,
  curiosity, or other scene-specific emotion;
- clothing detail that describes seams, closures, layers, wear, ornament,
  material weight, and cultural authorship.

Avoid:

- interchangeable fantasy-model faces;
- oversized heads, narrowed shoulders, weightless torsos, or decorative hands;
- youthfulness as the automatic beauty standard;
- costume complexity made from arbitrary straps, filigree, jewelry, or folds;
- facial detail that is sharp but not structurally modeled.

## Environment Standard

An environment should feel lived in rather than merely decorated. Useful
density comes from purposeful objects, signs of work, storage, circulation,
maintenance, personal habits, civic function, weather, and accumulated history.
It should create foreground, middle-ground, and background relationships while
preserving readable negative space.

Do not confuse lived-in density with clutter. Repeated bottles, books, candles,
particles, rubble, foliage, or tiny props are not environmental storytelling
unless their placement explains the place and its inhabitants.

## Material Detail Versus Visual Noise

Material detail explains what something is and how it was made, worn, handled,
or aged. Visual noise merely increases the number of marks.

Useful detail includes:

- garment seams, layered hems, closures, compression folds, embroidery, and
  wear at contact points;
- wood joints, plaster variation, tool marks, repaired masonry, tarnished
  metal, and objects placed for a believable task;
- concentrated facial, hand, and prop information at the narrative focus.

Reject detail that is uniformly distributed, procedurally repetitive, detached
from form, or added only to make the image appear expensive. Clothing may be
intricate without becoming oppressive when its detail follows construction,
silhouette, hierarchy, and material.

## What "Painterly" Means Here
- Forms are described with shape and value before texture.
- Not every surface is fully resolved.
- Backgrounds simplify with distance.
- Foliage is grouped into masses.
- Stone is suggested rather than individually engraved.
- Cloth is defined by major folds, silhouette, and light.
- Empty or quiet visual areas are allowed.
- Brush language should feel intentional rather than algorithmically noisy.

## Avoid
- Hyperrealism
- PBR material treatment
- Uniform micro-detail
- Excessive local contrast
- Dense particles, debris, dust, sparks, snow, leaves, or floating motes
- Geometric clothing artifacts
- Hard-light constructs unless canonically justified
- Plastic skin
- Excessive sharpening
- Flat generic anime rendering
- cheerful-adventure tone as an automatic default
- uniformly pleasant, youthful, or idealized expressions
- heavy dark contour lines carrying most of the form
- simplified animation surfaces with little painterly transition
- Dark palette as a default

## Maturity Is Not Grimness

“Mature” does not mean universally dark, violent, gritty, desaturated, solemn,
or photorealistic. Bright beauty, leisure, romance, humor, safety, ceremony,
color, and wonder remain essential. Maturity comes from dimensional rendering,
specific emotion, authored design, credible material weight, meaningful
contrast, and a world that can contain both delight and consequence.
